Supporting teens—and the parents who support them

Adolescence changes more than the child.

As teens develop greater independence, families often find that old ways of communicating, setting boundaries, or solving problems no longer work as well as they once did. A teenager may be struggling with anxiety, school pressure, friendships, identity, or independence while a parent is simultaneously wondering how much to intervene, what limits to set, and how to stay connected.

At Forward Focus Counseling, parenting counseling and adolescent therapy are connected by an understanding of the relationship between teens and the people who care for them.

Parenting counseling

When should I step in—and when should I give my teen space?

Parenting an adolescent can leave even experienced parents questioning what to do. You may be wondering how to set limits without creating constant conflict, how to respond to anxiety or school stress, or how to stay connected while allowing your teenager to become more independent.

Parenting counseling provides a space to understand what may be happening underneath the behavior, identify patterns that are not working, and develop practical ways to communicate, set boundaries, reduce conflict, and strengthen connection.

Your child does not necessarily need to be the person in therapy for things at home to begin changing. Sometimes supporting the parent is an effective place to start.

A therapist who understands teenagers—and the worlds they are navigating.

Adolescence brings rapid changes in friendships, family relationships, school expectations, identity, emotions, and independence. Therapy gives adolescents a space of their own to understand what they are experiencing, develop practical skills, and communicate their needs while recognizing the importance of their relationships with parents and family.

Parent-teen relationships

Sometimes the concern is what is happening between you.

Communication can deteriorate as parents become more worried and involved while teenagers seek more privacy and independence. Attempts to help can be interpreted as criticism or control, while a teenager’s withdrawal can leave parents feeling shut out.

Rachel helps families understand these patterns, improve communication, establish appropriate boundaries, and find ways for parents and adolescents to remain connected as their relationship changes.

17+ years supporting adolescents and families

Experience in the therapy room—and in schools.

Rachel’s extensive experience as a professional school counselor provides an understanding of adolescents within the environments where they spend much of their lives. School pressure, friendships, social dynamics, academic expectations, family relationships, independence, and emotional development often overlap.

Her collaborative approach draws from Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT), Internal Family Systems (IFS), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), attachment-based approaches, and family-systems perspectives.
